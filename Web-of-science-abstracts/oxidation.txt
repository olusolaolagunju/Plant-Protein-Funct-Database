## **1. What oxidation reaction is happening? (Simplified)**

During rice ageing, the fats in rice undergo **lipid oxidation**. One of the main oxidation products formed is **13‑HPODE**, a lipid hydroperoxide.

When rice protein is incubated with 13‑HPODE, it triggers **protein oxidation**, which mainly causes:

### **a. Protein carbonylation**
- 13‑HPODE reacts with amino acids in rice protein  
- This adds **carbonyl groups** (oxidation markers) to the protein  
- Carbonylation usually means the protein is damaged or structurally altered

Amino acids that form carbonyls upon oxidation:
Lysine → aminoadipic semialdehyde

Arginine → glutamic semialdehyde

Proline → glutamic semialdehyde

Threonine → amino ketone derivatives

2. Secondary carbonylation via lipid oxidation products (like 13‑HPODE)
This is the pathway relevant to your example:

13‑HPODE reacts with amino acids in rice protein → carbonyl groups appear
### **b. Loss of sulfhydryl (–SH) groups**
- 13‑HPODE oxidizes cysteine residues  
- –SH groups disappear because they form **disulfide bonds**  
- This leads to **protein crosslinking** and formation of aggregates
Lipid‑derived protein carbonylation requires the presence of lipids.
If you have a pure protein isolate with no lipids, this specific pathway cannot occur.

### **c. Changes in secondary structure**
Oxidation shifts the protein from flexible structures to more rigid ones:

- ↓ alpha‑helix  
- ↓ beta‑turn  
- ↓ random coil  
- ↑ beta‑sheet  

This is typical of oxidized proteins becoming more **ordered, rigid, and aggregated**.

### **d. Formation of soluble aggregates**
- Oxidation promotes protein–protein interactions  
- Aggregates form through disulfide bonds and hydrophobic interactions

## **2. Why did the researchers use 13‑HPODE? (The aim)**

The goal was to **simulate the natural oxidation that happens during rice ageing** and see **how lipid oxidation products affect rice protein functionality**.

### **In simple terms:**
They used 13‑HPODE to **mimic real-life oxidative stress** and study how rice protein changes when exposed to lipid oxidation products.

### **More specifically, they wanted to understand:**

- **How oxidation alters rice protein structure**  
  (carbonylation, loss of –SH groups, secondary structure changes, aggregation)

- **How these structural changes affect functional properties**  
  (solubility, WHC, OHC, foaming, emulsifying ability)

- **Whether mild oxidation can improve emulsification**  
  (it did — at low 13‑HPODE levels)

- **Whether severe oxidation damages functionality**  
  (it did — at high 13‑HPODE levels)

### **Why this matters:**
Rice protein functionality changes during storage and processing.  
Understanding oxidation helps:

- improve rice protein quality  
- control ageing effects  
- optimize rice‑based food formulations  
- predict shelf‑life behavior
