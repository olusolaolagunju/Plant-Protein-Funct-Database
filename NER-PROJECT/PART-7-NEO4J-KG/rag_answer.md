# Write a detailed overview of ultrasonication's effect on the physicochemical and structural properties of the plant protein sources represented in the data.

Ultrasonication has been shown to significantly impact the physicochemical and structural properties of plant proteins, with effects varying based on the frequency and combination with other treatments. Here's an overview based on the provided data:

### Effects of Ultrasonication Alone

1. **Physicochemical Properties:**
   - **Increased Solubility and Emulsification:** Ultrasonication generally increases solubility and emulsification activity across various plant proteins. For instance, it significantly improved the solubility of proteins like RPSPI and PMPI, with increases up to 32.1% (10-1016_j-ijbiomac-2025-143154_results_discussion1). Emulsification activity also increased, with notable improvements in emulsifying activity index (10-1016_j-foodchem-2024-138671_results_discussion2).
   - **Foaming and Water Holding Capacities:** Ultrasonication enhances foaming capacity and water holding capacity (WHC). For example, the WHC of treated samples increased significantly, reaching up to 145.54% (10-1007_s13197-020-04241-8_results_discussion1).
   - **Particle Size Reduction:** A consistent decrease in particle size was observed, which contributes to improved solubility and emulsification properties (10-1016_j-foodchem-2024-138671_abstract1).

2. **Structural Changes:**
   - **Protein Unfolding and Denaturation:** Ultrasonication induces protein unfolding and denaturation, leading to increased exposure of hydrophobic groups and free sulfhydryl groups, enhancing solubility and gel-forming capabilities (10-1016_j-foodhyd-2024-110672_conclusion1).
   - **Secondary Structure Modifications:** It causes changes in secondary structures, such as a decrease in α-helix and β-turn content while increasing β-sheet and random coil content (10-1016_j-ijbiomac-2025-143154_results_discussion1).

3. **Comparative Performance:**
   - **Versus High-Pressure Homogenization (HPH):** Ultrasonication outperformed HPH in enhancing emulsifying activity index and thermal stability (10-1016_j-ultsonch-2025-107321_conclusion1). However, HPH was more effective in reducing particle size and increasing solubility (10-1016_j-ultsonch-2025-107321_conclusion1).

### Combined Treatments

1. **With Enzymatic Hydrolysis:**
   - **Enhanced Solubility and Structural Changes:** The combination of enzymatic hydrolysis and ultrasonication increased solubility and altered secondary structures, such as increasing β-sheet content (10-1016_j-foodchem-2025-144247_results_discussion1).

2. **With pH Shifting:**
   - **Improved Functional Properties:** The combination of ultrasonication and pH shifting significantly improved solubility and gelation properties compared to individual treatments (10-1016_j-foodhyd-2024-110672_conclusion1).

3. **With TGase Cross-Linking:**
   - **Enhanced Gel Properties:** Ultrasonication combined with TGase cross-linking increased the storage modulus and apparent viscosity, indicating improved gelation effects (10-3390_gels9020091_results_discussion1).

### Specific Observations

- **Dual-Frequency Ultrasonication:** This method was particularly effective in reducing crystallinity and enhancing water absorption and oil holding capacities in pea flour (10-1016_j-ijbiomac-2026-152753_abstract1).
- **High-Intensity Ultrasonication:** It induced structural changes such as protein unfolding and increased emulsification activity, although it sometimes led to decreased foam stability (10-1016_j-ultsonch-2017-01-042_results_discussion1).

Overall, ultrasonication, especially when combined with other treatments, significantly enhances the functional and structural properties of plant proteins, making it a versatile tool in food science applications.
