RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. It is an AI architecture that connects Large Language Models (LLMs) to external data sources to retrieve accurate, up-to-date facts before generating an answer.

Here is how RAG works, why it matters, and where graph databases fit in:


1. The Core Problem RAG Solves
Standard LLMs suffer from two main limitations:


	Knowledge Cutoffs: They only know what was in their training dataset.

	Hallucinations: When asked about proprietary, complex, or unlearned data, they often make up convincing but false answers.

RAG fixes this by acting like an "open-book exam" for the AI. Instead of relying solely on memory, the model searches your database for relevant context first, feeds that context into the prompt, and then writes the answer based on those verified facts.


2. How standard RAG Works (Vector Search)
	Index: Your documents (PDFs, papers, texts) are chunked into small snippets and converted into numerical vectors (embeddings).

	Retrieve: When a user asks a question, the system searches the vector database for text snippets that are mathematically similar in meaning.

	Generate: The top matching snippets are appended to the LLM's prompt as context so it can synthesize the response.

3. Where Neo4j Comes In: GraphRAG
Standard vector RAG works well for simple text search, but it struggles with multi-step reasoning, complex dependencies, or structural logic (e.g., "Which extraction method decreased solubility under specific conditions?").

That is where GraphRAG (using Neo4j) upgrades the pipeline:


	Connected Context: Instead of retrieving isolated text chunks, the AI retrieves structured subgraphs—exact entities and their direct relationships.

	Multi-Hop Accuracy: The AI can trace chains of evidence (Node A → Relationship → Node B) without losing track of logic across multiple documents.

	Traceable Sources: Every claim generated by the LLM can be mapped back to a specific node, property, or relationship in your graph database for 100% verifiability.
